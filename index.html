Why we exist

Many families in Nigeria raise a child or care for a relative with an intellectual or developmental disability with little information, little support and a great deal of stigma.

Whole Person Ability Initiative exists to change that. We help families understand what they are facing, find the right health and education services, and be welcomed by their communities. Every person has abilities, and our job is to help those abilities be seen.

Understanding the conditions

  • Intellectual disability

    Significant differences in learning, reasoning and everyday skills that begin in childhood. With the right support, people with an intellectual disability learn and grow.

  • Down syndrome

    A genetic condition caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21. It happens by chance and is not caused by anything a parent did.

  • Autism

    A difference in how a person communicates, interacts and experiences the world. It is a spectrum, so every autistic person is different.

  • Developmental delay

    When a child reaches milestones such as walking or talking later than expected. Spotting it early means support can start sooner.

How you can support someone

  • Speak to the person, not about them.
  • Be patient and give them time.
  • Celebrate small steps.
  • Talk to a health professional early if you notice delays.
  • Include them in school, church and community life.

This is general information, not medical advice. Please speak to a doctor about your own child.

What we do

  • Awareness

    Talks and campaigns in schools, churches and communities that replace myths about disability with facts.

  • Family support

    Meetups and guidance for parents and caregivers, so no family has to face this alone.

  • Referrals

    Connecting families to health professionals, therapists and schools that can support their child.

  • Early signs

    Helping parents and teachers spot developmental delays early, when support can do the most good.

  • Inclusion

    Speaking up for the rights and dignity of people with disabilities, and building skills and livelihood opportunities.
